Gravitational atom beside hydrogen

Hydrogen, and a cloud of very light particles around a spinning black hole. Drawn at the same scale they have the same shapes and the same ladder of levels. Gravity does the pulling instead of electric charge, and the centre is a horizon that can feed the cloud or swallow it.

Exploration, not peer reviewed, no new result claimed. By Aska Digital with Claude (Anthropic) for Foundation0. Model, checks and open questions: Foundation-0/fractals. First study: the Witness Map.

Strength of the pull, Hydrogen's is fixed by nature: α = e²/(4πε₀ħc) = 1/137. A gravitational atom's is the hole's size over the particle's wavelength, so it can be anything.

The scale factor

Size: Bohr radius
Energy: spacing of the levels

Both ladders follow E = μc²(1 − α²/2n²): the particle's rest energy μc², less a binding energy that falls as 1/n². Drawn in units of each atom's own ground-state binding, the two ladders are the same.

Side by side

What is the same

  • One equation. Far from the hole, the wave of a light particle obeys the same Schrödinger equation as hydrogen's electron, with a pull that weakens as 1/r (Baumann, Chia and Porto 2019, BCP below, eq. 2.5). Only the strength α and the particle's mass μ change.
  • So the same solutions. Measured in its own Bohr radius a₀ = ħ/(μcα), each state has the same shape in both atoms. The two columns above are drawn from the same lines; only the labels differ.
  • The same ladder. Levels at μc²(1 − α²/2n²) in both (eq. 2.8), and each cloud is most likely found at n²a₀ from the centre (eq. 2.9).

What differs

    Why 211 and 322

      How it's computed

      Everything runs live in this page from gravitational-atom/atom.js, the same file npm test checks. It is the leading order in α: hydrogen's formulas with gravity's α.

      α = GMμ/(ħc) = rg/λca₀ = ħ/(μcα) = rg/α²En = μc²(1 − α²/2n²)r₊ = rg(1 + √(1 − χ²))grows if ω/m < ΩH = χc/(2r₊)

      Checks against published numbers

      • Hydrogen: 13.6057 eV and 0.529177 Å, the CODATA 2022 Rydberg energy and Bohr radius to 1 part in 10⁸. Letting the proton recoil gives 13.5983 eV against the measured 13.5984 eV; the gap is mostly fine structure, which this model leaves out.
      • Baumann, Chia and Porto 2019: α ≈ 0.02 for 3 Suns and 10⁻¹² eV (eq. 2.6); α ≈ 0.37 and gravitational waves at about 5 mHz for 5 million Suns and 10⁻¹⁷ eV (section 4.3); 484 Hz per 10⁻¹² eV (eq. 4.1); the 211 level grows only below α ≈ 0.25 at spin 0.8 (section 2.2); clouds peak at n²a₀ (eq. 2.9).
      • Growth rates: the 211 e-folding time is within 5× of Arvanitaki, Baryakhtar and Huang (2015, Table I) at α = 0.3, and 16× too slow at the fastest growth found numerically by Dolan (2007). Bosons in a grown cloud: 8×10⁷⁶ for 10 Suns, which Arvanitaki, Baryakhtar and Huang round to 10⁷⁶ (eq. 8).
      • The wavefunctions add up to one particle and match the textbook closed forms. The drawn lines sit on their density levels to 1 part in 10⁹.
      • All of these run as automated checks. Mistakes found along the way are in CORRECTIONS.md.

      What it leaves out

      • Everything past leading order. Corrections to the levels are of relative size about α² (BCP 2019, eq. 2.10), shown above for the current α. Near the hole, and for α above about 0.3, the true cloud is only roughly hydrogen-like; accurate clouds and growth rates need numerical work (for example Dolan 2007).
      • Accurate growth rates. The rates here are leading order (Detweiler 1980, with the factor of 2 corrected as noted in BCP 2019, eq. 2.12). They are good at small α but too slow near the fastest growth: about 4× at α = 0.3 against Arvanitaki, Baryakhtar and Huang (2015, Table I) and 16× at α = 0.42 against the numerical peak of Dolan (2007). Read the times as upper limits there.
      • The cloud's own gravity, the hole's small loss of mass as it spins down, the particles' interactions with each other, and anything else near the hole (gas, a companion star).
      • For a real field the density is not exactly a still doughnut: a small part rotates with the pattern, and that part radiates the gravitational waves (BCP 2019, section 2.3).
      • The centre is held fixed. In hydrogen the proton's recoil shifts the levels by about 1 part in 1,800; in the gravitational atom by μ/M, shown in the table.

# Scaling across cosmic time

The [gravity acceleration-evolution study](https://github.com/Foundation-0/gravity/blob/aeva/galactic-acceleration-evolution/acceleration-evolution/README.md) asks whether a galactic acceleration scale follows the expansion rate. It implements a₀(z) = A[H(z)/H₀]^q, compares constant and H-tracking cases, and checks them against published MUSE-DARK fitted curves.

## What this tests about repetition

The exponent q describes evolution with cosmic time. It is not a fractal dimension. A relation can retain a similar mathematical form while its dimensional normalization changes. Conversely, two objects can resemble one another while obeying different equations or boundary conditions.

For a meaningful scaling claim, specify the measured quantity, the transformation, the parameter that remains fixed, and the range over which the comparison holds. Here the transformation is a change in cosmic epoch. The candidate invariant under H tracking is a₀/H, after allowing a common amplitude. A scale tied to constant Λ gives a different history despite a possible numerical resemblance today.

The deep-regime relation Vflat⁴ = G Mbar a₀ provides a conditional observable test. It requires isolated systems, asymptotic flat velocities and total baryonic mass. Using a peak rotation speed or a stellar-only mass changes the question. The gravity study keeps those definitions separate and does not turn incompatible summaries into apparent evidence.

## The cosmological analogy

Particle–antiparticle annihilation has a known inverse: pair production. The [annihilation and relics note](https://github.com/Foundation-0/gravity/blob/aeva/galactic-acceleration-evolution/acceleration-evolution/annihilation-and-relics.md) distinguishes that established process from a proposed collision of universe-scale objects.

A repeated visual pattern alone supplies neither an interaction law nor a mechanism for producing new expanding universes.

No universal fractal law follows from an evolving a₀. The shared lesson is methodological: test a specified relation, state its domain, and preserve where it fails. The acceleration study belongs alongside this repository's bounded studies of quantum paths, gravitational atoms and cosmic clustering, without replacing their distinct physical assumptions.

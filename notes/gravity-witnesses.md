# Gravity-only witnesses for dark matter (issue 3)

Dark matter's only witness in the model is a black hole horizon. What other
gravity-only witnesses exist, and how strong are they?

## Candidates and bounds

1. **Gravitational scattering off passing stars and gas clouds.** A passing mass M
   at impact parameter b imprints a phase `dphi = G M m dx / (hbar v b)` on a
   particle of mass m superposed by dx. Integrating over encounters (saturated
   where phase >= 1) gives a rate. For the solar neighbourhood
   (n ~ 0.1 pc^-3 stars, M ~ 1 Msun, v ~ 200 km/s, out to ~1 kpc):

   - Fuzzy (wave) dark matter, m = 1e-22 eV: W ~ 10^-7 at a 1 kpc gap, ~10^-62
     at 1 um. Negligible. The "no witnesses" claim holds for wave dark matter.
   - Particle (WIMP) dark matter, m = 100 GeV: W ~ 10^42 at a 1 kpc gap.
     Stellar gravity is a strong witness at galactic scales; heavy dark matter
     superpositions cannot persist across kpc separations.

   Gas clouds (GMCs) give comparable or smaller rates.

2. **Soft graviton emission without a horizon.** A particle in a superposition
   is not accelerating in the sense needed for gravitational radiation; any
   emission rate scales as G m^2 omega^3 / (hbar c^5) with omega ~ hbar/(m dx^2),
   which is astronomically small for both masses. Negligible.

3. **Tidal fields of the galaxy.** A classical tidal field is a c-number: it
   does not become entangled with the particle and records no which-path
   information. Only quantized (graviton) tides would, which is case 2.
   Negligible as a witness.

## Conclusion

Only stellar gravitational scattering matters, and only for particle (heavy)
dark matter at galactic (kpc) separations. Wave (fuzzy) dark matter retains
no gravity-only witnesses besides a black hole horizon, so the model's "dark
matter has no witnesses" statement applies to wave dark matter specifically.
